Dengue genomic surveillance coverage

Open dengue sequences per country and year, against a minimum of 59 sequences a year for each circulating serotype, enough to find a lineage that makes up 5% of it.

Methods, the derivation of the target and the full data are in Bermann T, et al. Open genomic surveillance of dengue virus against reported and modeled burden, worldwide, 2001–2026. In preparation.

Why 59?

59 open sequences give a 95% chance of including a lineage that makes up 5% of one serotype in a season (1 − 0.9559 ≥ 0.95). It is a floor, not a goal. Each circulating serotype needs its own 59. Where no serotype reached 59 the country falls short whatever circulated, and it is colored by its most-sequenced serotype. Where one did, the floor is met only if every other circulating serotype also has 59, and which serotypes circulated is known from case typing only for Brazil (SINAN, by region); elsewhere the cell stays amber. Brazil is shown by region and other countries as a whole, so the comparison is not like for like. Open sequences are public dengue virus sequences of at least 505 bp in Pathoplexus (about 5% of the genome), the coverage recommended for lineage assignment.
