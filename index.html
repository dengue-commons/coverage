Dengue genomic surveillance coverage

Open dengue sequences per country and year, against a minimum of 59 sequences a year for each circulating serotype, enough to detect a lineage at 5%.

Methods, the derivation of the target and the full data are in Bermann T, et al. Open genomic surveillance of dengue virus against reported and modeled burden, worldwide, 2001–2026. In preparation.

Why 59?

59 is the smallest random sample that contains at least one sequence from a lineage carried by 5% of infections, with 95% confidence (1 − 0.9559 ≥ 0.95). It is a floor, not a goal. Each serotype in circulation needs its own 59, so a country is colored by its least-sequenced serotype. A serotype counts as circulating when it makes up at least 10% of the open sequences that year; in Brazil, when SINAN reports cases of it in the region. Brazil is shown by region because a national total can hide regions with almost no sequences. Open sequences are public dengue virus sequences of at least 505 bp in Pathoplexus (about 5% of the genome), the genome coverage recommended for lineage assignment.
